HOW IT WORKS - Rose
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The whole artwork is one Python file. It works in six steps.

1. The plant
   The stem, two leaves and five sepals are built from thousands of
   points in 3D space. Each point also knows which way its surface faces
   (its "normal"), so it can be lit.

2. The flower
   The petals are one spiral surface that curls inward like a real rose.
   At the start it is blended into a closed, egg-shaped bud; as the
   bloom value rises from 0 to 1 the bud loosens, the outer petals lean
   away first and the heart stays tight.

3. How it appears
   Every point gets a number for "when it is drawn": the stem from the
   ground up, then the leaves, the sepals, and the bud from its base to
   its tip. A bright point of light travels up the stem, throwing sparks.

4. Light and motion
   - A key light from above and a rim light that slowly circles the rose.
   - Brightness = how much a surface faces the light plus a sharp highlight;
     the heart of the flower is a little darker, for depth.
   - The rose sways a little; the lower outer petals trail behind the sway.
   - Dewdrops sparkle on the petals, and a few of them swell, fall with
     gravity and splash on the floor.
   - Twinkling dust drifts around the flower.

5. From points to letters
   The screen is a grid of letter cells. For each cell:
   - the nearest point wins,
   - edges become  | / \ - _  following the outline,
   - the inside becomes letters chosen by brightness (dim: 1 i l ; bright: 8 B $ #),
   - bright highlights become  *  and the brightest throw four-pointed rays.

6. The window
   Each cell is drawn as a small glyph in its colour, a soft glow is added,
   and the picture updates up to 30 times a second.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   bloom()   how fast the bud opens
   DRAW      when the plant starts and finishes appearing
   COLORS    the colour of each kind of point
   W, H      window size
