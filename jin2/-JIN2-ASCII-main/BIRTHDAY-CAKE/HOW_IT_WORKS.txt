HOW IT WORKS - Birthday Cake
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The whole artwork is one Python file. It works in six steps.

1. The cake
   A slice of a round cake is built from thousands of points in 3D space:
   two cut faces with two sponge layers and a band of cream between them,
   a frosted outer side, a frosted top with a piped ridge and a swirl,
   a twisted candle, sprinkles, and a plate with a scalloped gold rim.
   Each point also knows which way its surface faces (its "normal"),
   so it can be lit.

2. How it appears
   Every point gets a number for "when it is drawn", mostly by height,
   so the cake builds from the plate up. A few points fly in from
   further away before they settle.

3. Light
   - A key light from above gives the basic shading.
   - When the candle is lit, a second, warm light sits just above the
     flame. It fades quickly with distance, so only the part of the cake
     near the candle brightens, and it flickers with the flame.
   - The flame itself is a teardrop of points that sways and flickers,
     with a bright core and a soft halo around it.

4. From points to letters
   The screen is a grid of letter cells. For each cell:
   - the nearest point wins,
   - edges become  | / \ - _  following the outline,
   - the inside gets letters that suit the material:
     o 0 a e for the sponge, ~ for cream and frosting, = for the crust.

5. Confetti and the words
   - Confetti pieces fall and spin, drawn as  - / | \  in bright colours.
   - HAPPY and BIRTHDAY are drawn as big letters, then filled with
     small code letters. They pop in, settle, and catch a twinkle now
     and then.

6. The window
   Each cell is drawn as a small glyph in its colour, a soft glow is added,
   and the picture updates up to 30 times a second.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   BUILD     how long the cake takes to appear
   flicker() how much the flame moves
   COLORS    the colour of each kind of point
   W, H      window size
