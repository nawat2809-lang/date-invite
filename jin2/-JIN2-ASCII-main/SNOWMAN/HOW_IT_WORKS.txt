HOW IT WORKS - Don't Melt
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The whole artwork is one Python file. A little 3D snowman is built from
points, seen through a fixed camera, and every cell of the screen
becomes a letter.

1. The snowman
   Three blocks of snow, 12, 10 and 8 voxels wide, stacked from large to
   small. Each block is covered in points. Eyes, smile and buttons are
   voxels on the front face that are left out, so they show up as black
   squares. Two sticks make the arms, and one of them twitches now and then.

2. Building in
   At the start the letters gather from the bottom up. One point in ten
   flies in from the dark, the rest fade in where they belong.

3. Snowfall
   420 flakes drift down around him at different speeds. Nearer flakes
   are brighter.

4. Melting
   Twelve times, a small chunk of snow comes loose, top edges first, left
   and right taking turns. Each piece falls with real gravity, bounces
   twice, skids until friction stops it, and melts into a blue puddle.
   His face and buttons never fall off. As he melts he gets a little
   shorter and wider at the base, and the temperature on screen rises.

5. From points to letters
   Points are drawn far to near, so nearer ones paint over farther ones.
   Brighter surfaces get denser letters (from : and ; up to 8 B $ #).
   Outlines use / | \ - _ following the edge direction.
   Falling flakes are . + *, the puddle is ~.
   Each frame fades into the next and a soft glow is added on top.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   EYES, SMILE     where his face squares sit
   THUDS           when each chunk falls
   FLAKES          how much snow falls
   TAG_COLOR       the colours
