HOW IT WORKS - I Love You
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The whole artwork is one Python file. A heart is covered in rows of
"I love you" in 114 languages, and the letters alone make its shape.

1. The phrases
   Each phrase was drawn once as a picture, because scripts like Arabic,
   Hindi or Thai need their letters joined and shaped correctly.
   Right-to-left languages are typed in from the right.

2. The outline
   The right half of the heart is a list of 48 points. It is mirrored
   and filled to get the whole shape.

3. Rows of text
   The heart is cut into rows. In each row, phrases are picked so they
   fill the row exactly from edge to edge, like justified text. Only
   whole phrases are used, so the edge of the heart is made of letters.
   The front and the back get different phrases.

4. Depth
   Every point inside the heart gets a thickness: flat sides all round,
   faces that bulge towards the middle, and a slightly raised rim just
   inside the edge. The sides carry short phrases too.

5. Turning and light
   The heart turns to the right around its vertical axis, once every
   10 seconds. For every row, the front, the back and the sides are
   turned and drawn far to near. The colour of each letter comes from
   a simple chrome reflection: a bright sky above, a dark band in the
   middle and a pink floor below, so the shine slides across the
   letters as the heart turns.

6. The start
   In the first 3 seconds the phrases are typed in one by one, all over
   the heart. Each frame fades into the next and a soft glow is added.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   WIDTH           size of the heart
   WALL, DOME      how thick and how puffy it is
   TURN            seconds for one turn
   SKY, BAND, FLOOR   the colours
